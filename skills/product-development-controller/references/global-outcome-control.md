# Global Outcome Control

## Purpose

Use this reference whenever a material local action could become detached from the approved Outcome or the credible end-to-end route.

The governing principle is:

> **A collection of local optima is not necessarily a global optimum. Prefer the best credible route to the Outcome over making every local step individually perfect.**

AI is often strong at improving the object directly in front of it. That strength becomes a control risk when the current implementation, experiment, laboratory, test harness, architecture, process, metric, or tool is optimized beyond what the Outcome requires.

This is a cross-mode control guardrail. It does not create a new Kernel concept, Mode, Gate, lifecycle state, role, artifact class, scheduler, or subsystem.

## Global Outcome check

Before a material local optimization, establish proportionately:

1. **Outcome** — what approved result should ultimately become true?
2. **Current position** — which Workpath waypoint, Primary Unknown, Engineering boundary, or delivery condition currently gates progress?
3. **Route contribution** — how can this action change the route position, the next product decision, or the trustworthy delivery claim?
4. **Decision-sufficient threshold** — how good, real, complete, reliable, or rigorous must this local result be for that purpose?
5. **Accepted imperfection** — what may remain incomplete, approximate, manual, ugly, indirect, or temporarily wrong without invalidating the decision or delivery claim?
6. **Stop / switch condition** — when will local optimization stop so work can advance, change method, or replan?

The answer may be compact for inexpensive reversible work. Make it explicit when cost, iteration count, rigor, scope, or Product Owner concern is rising.

A local action is not justified merely because it improves a local metric or professional standard. It must advance the active route, obtain decision-relevant evidence, remove a real blocker, preserve a required boundary, or establish that replanning is necessary.

## Necessary detours and local imperfection

A locally imperfect or indirect step can be globally correct.

Accept a detour when:

- its contribution to the end-to-end route is credible;
- a more direct route is unavailable, riskier, or less informative;
- its cost and limitations are understood;
- it has a stop or return condition;
- it does not silently become a separate advancing Work Focus.

Do not reject a necessary detour merely because immediate visible progress or a local quality score becomes worse. Conversely, do not call drift a detour: repeated work with no credible route contribution must be redirected or replanned.

## Means-end inversion

Treat the following as material drift signals:

- the implementation, experiment, laboratory, benchmark, test harness, architecture, workflow, process, metric, or tool is being perfected as though it were the product;
- quality or evidence standards keep rising without a causal reason that could change the decision;
- the team can explain how the local object improved but cannot state what product decision, route position, or user-visible Outcome changed;
- most effort repairs non-causal defects or future concerns while the active waypoint stalls;
- inability to obtain an ideal environment is treated as evidence that the product direction is impossible, even though a lower-fidelity or alternative route remains credible;
- sunk cost, elegance, completeness, or technical pride becomes the main reason to continue;
- the Product Owner repeatedly has to ask what is being done, where the project is, and why the current action serves the goal.

When a signal is confirmed:

1. restate the Outcome and current route position;
2. identify the exact decision or delivery claim that remains unsupported;
3. compare the current method with a cheaper, lower-fidelity, or differently structured credible method;
4. reduce the local standard to the decision-sufficient threshold when protected boundaries allow;
5. preserve limitations instead of trying to erase every imperfection;
6. collect evidence, advance, switch method, stop, or explicitly replan as appropriate.

Do not answer means-end inversion with more process or a more elaborate control system.

## Preview: decision-sufficient experiments

A Preview experiment exists to support a practical product decision about one Primary Unknown. It does not exist to become a flawless research environment.

Before Preview implementation or laboratory repair, state:

- the practical decision the evidence will inform;
- the one Primary Unknown;
- the Minimum Credible Fidelity for causal dimensions;
- the decision-sufficient evidence threshold;
- accepted imperfections, approximations, manual steps, weak controls, artificial conditions, sample limitations, and non-causal defects;
- the stop or switch condition.

Use paper-grade, benchmark-grade, production-grade, or exhaustive rigor only when that rigor is causal to the decision or required by a protected specialist boundary.

When a rough setup can credibly answer the question, use it and limit the conclusion. When the ideal setup is unavailable, distinguish:

- **no credible answer is possible because a causal dimension is missing or distorted**; from
- **the setup is not ideal but remains decision-useful with explicit limitations**.

The second case is not a reason to abandon the product direction. Prefer a manual, approximate, lower-fidelity, or alternative experiment when it can still resolve the Primary Unknown.

Once decision-sufficient evidence exists, stop improving the laboratory and make the product decision. More rigor is useful only when it has enough expected decision value to justify its cost.

## Explore

In Explore, research and analysis are means to reduce decision-driving uncertainty. Stop when another source of evidence is stronger or when additional information is unlikely to change the route. Do not turn research completeness into the Outcome.

## Engineering

In Engineering, global Outcome control chooses and challenges the route before or through authorized revision. It does not silently weaken an authoritative frozen completion boundary.

When an Engineering boundary no longer serves the approved Outcome, use the governing revision path. Do not bypass tests, review, Product Owner acceptance, specialist judgment, security, privacy, compliance, data integrity, deployment safety, or irreversible-operation protections by invoking “global optimization.”

## Product Owner visibility

When route visibility is materially useful, explain in product language:

`overall Outcome -> current position -> why this action -> sufficient standard -> accepted imperfection -> stop/switch condition -> one next action`

The Product Owner should not have to continuously police local optimization. Repeated questions about what is happening, progress, or purpose are evidence that PDC must recover and clarify the route or interrupt drift.
